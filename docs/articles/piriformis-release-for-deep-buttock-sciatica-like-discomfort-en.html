What's Actually Happening in There

The piriformis is a small, stubborn muscle buried deep in the pelvis. People who sit for long hours, runners, and even those who sleep on one side too often can develop significant tension there. It's not glamorous anatomy, but understanding it changes how you approach the massage. Once you know where this muscle lives, you can work with it instead of just rubbing around it.

How I Work the Piriformis in My Sessions

I always start with broad strokes across the gluteal area to warm the tissue before going deeper. Then I use my elbow — carefully, with constant client feedback — to sink into the piriformis belly in a slow, sustained way. I hold, I breathe, I wait for the tissue to respond. Rushing this step accomplishes nothing; the muscle has to decide to let go.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I do piriformis massage on myself at home?

Yes, using a tennis ball or a firm foam roller placed under the buttock works well for self-pressure. Sit on it, find the tender spot, and breathe through gentle sustained pressure for 30–60 seconds. It takes practice but is very doable.

How often should I get this type of massage done?

In my experience, two sessions per week for two to three weeks tends to show noticeable progress for most people. After that, once every two to three weeks for maintenance is usually enough.

Is Artrovex cream safe for sensitive skin?

I'd always recommend doing a small patch test on your inner forearm first and waiting 24 hours before full application. I haven't had a client react badly to it, but individual skin sensitivity varies and a patch test is the responsible step.
