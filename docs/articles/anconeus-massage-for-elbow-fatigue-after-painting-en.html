My Elbow Was Done After a Weekend of Painting — Here's What I Did About It

Last spring I repainted two rooms in my apartment over a long weekend. By Sunday evening my elbow felt like it had played three full matches back to back. I'm a sports coach, I know muscles — but I completely forgot about the anconeus until it reminded me itself.

What Is the Anconeus and Why Does Painting Exhaust It?

The anconeus is a small triangular muscle sitting just behind your elbow. It helps stabilize the joint during forearm rotation — exactly the motion you repeat hundreds of times while rolling paint or cutting edges with a brush. Most people never think about it until it becomes tight and heavy after hours of overhead or extended-arm work. It's small, but it carries a serious load.

How I Massage the Anconeus After a Long Session

I sit down, bend my elbow to about 90 degrees, and use my opposite thumb to find the small muscle mass just lateral to the olecranon — the bony tip of your elbow. Slow circular pressure for two to three minutes, working from the edges toward the center. I follow that with a few gentle forearm rotations to keep the tissue loose. No rushing. This is not a race.

Adding a Warming Cream to the Routine

A dry thumb on a tired elbow does not feel great, and friction alone is not enough. That's when I started incorporating a warming massage cream into the routine. The warmth helps me feel the muscle more clearly under my fingers, and the skin stays comfortable throughout the session. It turned a functional habit into something I actually look forward to doing.
