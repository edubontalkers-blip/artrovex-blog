My Cheeks Were Killing Me After a Wedding Weekend — Here's What Actually Helped

Last autumn I shot a friend's wedding as a side favour — three days of grinning, laughing, and posing for group photos. By Sunday night my cheekbones ached in a way I had never experienced before. That was the moment I started taking the zygomaticus muscle seriously as a real source of facial tension.

What Is the Zygomaticus and Why Does It Get Tight?

The zygomaticus major runs diagonally from your cheekbone down to the corner of your mouth — it is the muscle that pulls your lips into a smile. Most people never think about it because, well, smiling feels good. But sustained or forced smiling — think long presentations, photo sessions, or customer-facing work — can leave that muscle genuinely fatigued. The tension often radiates into the jaw and even the temples.

A Simple Zygomaticus Stretch You Can Do Right Now

Sit upright, relax your face completely, and let your mouth hang slightly open. Place two clean fingertips gently on each cheekbone and apply very light downward pressure while simultaneously pulling the corners of your mouth slightly inward — not a frown, just a soft neutral. Hold for eight to ten seconds and repeat three times on each side. I personally do this after every long client session and it has become a non-negotiable part of my wind-down routine.

Adding Gentle Massage to Support the Stretch

After the stretch I like to add a small amount of massage cream to the cheekbone area and work in slow circular movements toward the ear. This combination — stretch first, then massage — lets the muscle respond more openly to the manual pressure. My personal opinion is that skipping the massage step makes the stretch only half as effective. It takes maybe four extra minutes and the difference in how my face feels the next morning is noticeable.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can smiling really cause muscle tension?

Yes — like any muscle, the zygomaticus can fatigue from sustained use. It is less common than neck or shoulder tension but very real, especially after events involving long periods of forced or held smiling.

How often should I do the zygomaticus stretch?

Daily is fine if you have an active social or professional life. Even two to three times a week will make a difference if your tension is mild.

Is it safe to use a massage cream on the face?

Check the product label — Artrovex is designed as a cosmetic cream for external use. Always do a small patch test on your inner wrist first and avoid eye contact.
