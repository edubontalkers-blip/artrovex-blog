My Big Toe Was Killing My Morning Flow — Here's What I Started Doing

Last spring, I stepped off my mat after a vinyasa class and felt this sharp, stubborn tightness right under my big toe — like something was locked in place. I teach yoga six days a week in Milan, and I had never paid attention to those tiny sesamoid bones sitting beneath the first metatarsal until that moment. That one uncomfortable step started a whole personal journey into foot mobility I never expected.

What Even Are Sesamoid Bones and Why Do They Get Stuck

The sesamoids are two small bones embedded in the tendons beneath your big toe joint, and they help distribute force when you push off the ground. When they lose their glide — from tight footwear, repetitive yoga poses, or just daily walking patterns — that push-off moment feels stiff, sometimes even crunchy. I noticed mine most during warrior poses and walking barefoot on cold mornings. My personal opinion: most people have no idea these tiny bones exist until they cause problems, which is honestly a design flaw in how we teach body awareness.

The Mobilization Routine I Built Into My Morning

I start sitting on the floor with one foot across my opposite thigh — a simple cross-legged prep position. I use my thumb and index finger to gently hold the big toe joint and slowly circle the toe, coaxing movement in all directions without forcing anything. Then I press gently under the ball of the foot and do small back-and-forth glides with the skin over those bones — this is the part that really woke things up for me. I do this for about two to three minutes per foot before I even think about standing.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I do this mobilization routine even if my toe is not in pain?

Absolutely — I actually think preventive movement work is where this routine shines most. You do not need discomfort as a starting point; stiffness often builds quietly before it announces itself.

How long before I notice a difference in my push-off movement?

Honestly, it depends on how long the stiffness has been building. For me, I felt a small but real change in glide after about a week of daily work, but I have talked to students who needed three weeks of consistency.

Can I use Artrovex cream every day?
