My Upper Back Was a Mess After Years of Desk Work — Here's What Actually Helped

Last winter I caught my reflection in a shop window on Via del Corso and barely recognized my own posture. That moment pushed me back to the basics of spinal mobility work I had quietly abandoned.

Why Your Thoracic Spine Suffers Most From Sitting

The thoracic spine — that middle stretch of your back between your neck and lower ribs — is meant to extend, rotate, and breathe. Prolonged sitting collapses it forward. Over months, the muscles around those vertebrae shorten and stiffen, and your ribs literally have less room to expand. It is not dramatic at first, just a dull ache and a creeping forward tilt you stop noticing.

Three Drills I Use Every Morning Before Coffee

The first is the foam roller thoracic extension: lie over a roller placed horizontally across your mid-back, arms crossed over your chest, and gently let gravity do the work for thirty seconds at three different heights. Second is the open book stretch — lie on your side, knees stacked, and slowly rotate your top arm open toward the ceiling while keeping your hips still. Third is the seated thoracic rotation: sit upright in a chair, cross your arms, and rotate your torso left and right ten times each side. I do all three in sequence, six days a week.

The Role of Soft Tissue Work Before and After Drilling

Cold muscles resist. Warm muscles respond. Before I drill, I spend about four minutes doing gentle cat-cow movements just to wake up the spine's range of motion. After the drills, I focus on the paraspinal muscles — those long bands running alongside the vertebrae — because they tighten defensively during extended sitting. Addressing the soft tissue before and after makes the drills noticeably more effective over time.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long before I notice a difference in my posture from these drills?

In my personal experience and from coaching others, consistent daily practice for two to three weeks tends to bring the first noticeable change in how freely your upper back moves. Posture itself shifts more slowly — think months, not days — but the sensation of stiffness usually eases earlier than most people expect.

Can I do thoracic extension drills if I already have some upper back discomfort?

I always tell my clients to check with a qualified professional before starting any new physical practice if they have existing discomfort — that is not me being overly cautious, it is just sensible. Mild everyday stiffness from sitting is different from structural issues, and only someone who can actually assess you in person can make that distinction.
