What Is the Iliacus and Why Does It Get So Tight?

The iliacus sits inside your pelvis, hugging the inner bowl of the hip bone. It works alongside the psoas to pull your femur up — every time you sit, climb stairs, or sprint, it's working. The problem is that most of us sit for hours, which keeps this muscle in a shortened position all day long. Over time, that shortening becomes the muscle's default state, and you feel it as that stubborn deep front hip tightness.

How to Actually Release the Iliacus (Step by Step)

Lie on your back with knees bent. Place two or three fingers just inside your hip bone (ASIS) and slowly sink straight down — not toward your belly button, but straight into the floor. Hold light pressure there and breathe; you should feel a dense, slightly uncomfortable sensation that slowly softens over 60–90 seconds. Do this on both sides, then follow up with a slow hip flexor stretch while keeping your core gently engaged.
