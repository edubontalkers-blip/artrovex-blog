Why Your Shoulder Blade Sticks Out — And What I Do About It Every Morning

I was 34 when my left shoulder blade started winging out like a little fin. I didn't notice it myself — my training partner pointed it out mid-session, and honestly, it embarrassed me. Years of competitive rowing had left my serratus anterior completely neglected, buried under layers of dominant chest and lat work.

What Is the Serratus Anterior and Why Does It Go Quiet

The serratus anterior is a fan-shaped muscle that wraps around your ribcage and holds your shoulder blade flat against your back. When it stops firing properly, the blade lifts away from the ribs — what people call winging. In my experience coaching athletes of all ages here in Rome, this muscle is almost always the most ignored one in the upper body. It doesn't look impressive in the mirror, so nobody trains it.

The Activation Sequence I Use Every Single Morning

I start with wall push-plus: palms flat on the wall, elbows straight, then I push the wall away by rounding my upper back — not bending my elbows. I hold that end position for three seconds, release slowly, and repeat ten times. After that, I move to a serratus punch lying on my back: I hold a light dumbbell straight up, then reach it toward the ceiling by lifting the shoulder blade off the floor. This one is humble-looking but brutal after set three.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long before I see my shoulder blade sitting flatter?

That depends entirely on how consistent you are. In my coaching work, athletes who do the serratus activation daily for four to six weeks usually notice a visible difference in posture photos. Some people see changes in two weeks, others take longer — the muscle needs repetition, not intensity.

Can I do these exercises if I already have shoulder discomfort?

I always tell people to check with a physiotherapist or doctor before starting any new movement routine if something already feels off. I'm a coach and a former athlete — I'm not a clinician, and individual situations vary too much for me to give you a safe blanket answer.
