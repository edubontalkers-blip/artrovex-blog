My Wrists Were Screaming After 6 Hours of Typing — Here's What Actually Helped

Last winter I spent three straight days writing my training manual, hunched over my laptop like I'd forgotten everything I ever taught my athletes. By day two, my wrists felt like they'd been wrung out like a wet towel. I'm 48, I've coached bodies for over a decade, and I still made this mistake — so I know exactly how you feel right now.

Why Typing Wrecks Your Wrist Flexors Specifically

Your wrist flexors run along the inside of your forearm and they work constantly when you type, even when it feels like you're doing nothing. Hours of small, repetitive finger movements keep these muscles in a low-grade contraction for far too long. Unlike a hard workout, there's no obvious burn to warn you. The fatigue just accumulates quietly until one day you notice your wrists aching even at rest.

Three Wrist Flexor Stretches That I Actually Use

First: extend one arm straight in front of you, palm facing up, and gently pull your fingers downward with your other hand — hold for 20 seconds, switch sides. Second: place both palms flat on a desk, fingers pointing toward you, and slowly lean back until you feel the pull along the inside of your forearms. Third: make a gentle fist, then slowly open your hand wide and spread your fingers — repeat ten times per hand. In my personal opinion, that third one is the most underrated and most skipped, which is a shame.

Building a Recovery Habit Around Your Work Day

I tell every person I coach the same thing: set a phone timer for every 45 minutes when you're at a keyboard. When it goes off, stop, stretch, breathe. It takes less than two minutes and it changes everything over weeks. The discipline is boring — but boring is what works.
