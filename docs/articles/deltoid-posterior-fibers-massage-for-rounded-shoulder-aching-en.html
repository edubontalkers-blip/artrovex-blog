My Rounded Shoulders Were Killing Me After Training — Here's What Actually Helped

Last winter, after a long session reviewing training videos hunched over my laptop, I stood up and felt that familiar deep pull across the back of my shoulder — not sharp, just relentless. I've had that feeling on and off since my competitive years in Rome, and I know exactly where it lives: the posterior fibers of the deltoid, that curved muscle wrapping around the back of your shoulder. Most people ignore this spot completely, and I think that's a mistake.

What Are the Posterior Deltoid Fibers and Why Do They Get So Unhappy

The deltoid has three distinct portions — anterior, lateral, and posterior. The posterior fibers sit at the back of the shoulder and are responsible for extending and externally rotating the arm. When your shoulders round forward — whether from desk work, heavy pressing in the gym, or years of sport — these fibers get chronically overstretched and underworked. They don't scream at you immediately; they just build a slow, dull ache that makes reaching behind your back feel like a negotiation.

The Self-Massage Approach I Actually Use

I cross my right arm across my chest to expose the back of my left shoulder, then use my right hand's fingers to press into that meaty area behind the shoulder joint. Small, firm circles — not brutal, just deliberate. I spend about 90 seconds on each side, focusing on the spots that feel tight rather than the ones that feel fine. After years of coaching athletes, I genuinely believe this two-minute routine, done consistently before stretching, makes the stretch itself far more effective.

Frequently Asked Questions

How often should I massage the posterior deltoid area?

In my experience, 4 to 5 times per week is plenty for maintenance. Daily if you're in a heavy training block or sitting at a desk all day — but always listen to your body and skip a day if the area feels irritated rather than just tight.

Can I do this massage without any cream at all?

Technically yes, but dry friction on skin over a bony shoulder area gets uncomfortable quickly. A warming cream like Hondrocream makes the pressure more comfortable and helps your fingers move smoothly without dragging.

Is rounded shoulder posture something massage alone can fix?

No — and anyone telling you otherwise is oversimplifying. Massage like this helps address the muscular tension component, but you also need consistent stretching of the anterior shoulder, strengthening of the posterior chain, and honest changes to how you sit and move daily.

Give your posterior deltoid the attention it's been asking for — two minutes, some warmth, and a little consistency go a long way.
