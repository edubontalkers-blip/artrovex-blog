Why the Top of My Foot Aches After Long Walks — And What I Do About It

Last summer I walked nearly 18,000 steps guiding a client through a trail in the Grunewald, and by kilometer twelve my right foot was screaming at me from the top — not the sole, not the heel, the top. I had no idea what was happening until I got home and started pressing around and realized the culprit was a muscle I'd honestly underestimated for years: the extensor hallucis longus. That one experience completely changed how I approach foot care for myself and every client I work with.

What Is the Extensor Hallucis Longus and Why Does It Get So Tired?

The extensor hallucis longus is a long, thin muscle that runs from your lower leg all the way down to your big toe, and its job is to lift that toe off the ground with every single step. On a normal day that sounds manageable, but after hours of walking — especially on uneven terrain or in shoes with a tight toe box — it accumulates tension you can actually feel as a dull ache or tightness across the top of your foot. Most people rub the bottom of their feet after a long day and completely forget this overworked structure on top. I think that's one of the most common and most fixable mistakes in basic self-care routines.

How I Actually Massage This Muscle — Step by Step

Sit down with your foot resting on the opposite knee, and use your thumb to locate the tendon that runs from your ankle toward your big toe — you can feel it pop up when you flex your toe upward. Slide your thumb slowly along that tendon from the base of your big toe up toward the ankle, applying gentle, steady pressure without pressing so hard it becomes sharp pain. I usually do three passes, then I switch to small circular movements right at the muscle belly near the lower shin, where tension tends to pool after long walks. Take your time — this is not a race.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long should I massage the extensor hallucis longus?

Around 5 to 8 minutes per foot is usually enough — focus on quality of pressure rather than speed, and stop immediately if you feel sharp or shooting sensations.

Can I do this massage every day?

Yes, daily is fine if you're walking a lot — just keep the pressure moderate and listen to your foot; some days it needs lighter touch than others.

Is Artrovex cream safe to use on the top of the foot?

It's a cosmetic massage cream designed for external skin use, so apply it to intact skin only and avoid any broken or irritated areas.
