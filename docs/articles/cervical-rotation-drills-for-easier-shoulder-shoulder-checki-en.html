Cubital Tunnel Stretches: What I Do When My Clients' Fingers Go Numb

Last Tuesday, a graphic designer sat down on my table and immediately said her pinky and ring finger had been buzzing for three weeks. I recognized it right away — that classic ulnar nerve irritation from a compressed cubital tunnel, usually from long hours at a desk with bent elbows. I've seen this pattern so many times that I've built a small routine around it.

What's Actually Happening at the Elbow

The cubital tunnel is a narrow passage on the inner side of your elbow where the ulnar nerve runs close to the surface. When you keep your elbow bent for extended periods — typing, sleeping on your arm, leaning on a hard surface — that nerve gets compressed. The tingling or numbness you feel in the two outer fingers is the nerve signaling stress. It's not dangerous in mild cases, but it's uncomfortable and surprisingly easy to address with consistent stretching.

Three Stretches I Actually Use in My Practice

The first one is simple: extend your arm straight, then gently curl your wrist and fingers toward your body while keeping the elbow straight — hold for 20 seconds. The second involves bending the elbow fully, then wrapping your fingers around it to add slight traction; this one gets dramatic reactions from clients. The third is a nerve glide — alternating between elbow extended with wrist flexed, then elbow bent with wrist extended, slowly and smoothly about ten times. None of these should hurt; if they do, stop and check with a doctor.

How I Prepare the Arm Before Stretching

Before I ask a client to do any of these movements, I spend a few minutes on the surrounding soft tissue — the forearm flexors, the triceps, and the area just above and below the elbow crease. Warmed-up tissue responds better and the stretch feels less aggressive. I personally find that applying a quality massage cream before manual work makes a real difference in how the skin and underlying muscle respond. It's a preparation step, not a shortcut.
