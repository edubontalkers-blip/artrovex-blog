My Semimembranosus Massage Routine That Got Me Back on the Training Field

I remember sitting on the physio bench three years ago, grabbing the back of my knee and thinking — what on earth is pulling in there? It was not the obvious hamstring belly. It was deeper, closer to the inner knee, and every time I tried to bend my leg past 90 degrees it felt like a rope about to snap. That specific tightness, I later learned, often comes from the semimembranosus — the most medial of the hamstring muscles, and frankly the most ignored one in most self-care routines.

Where Exactly Is the Semimembranosus and Why Does It Get So Tight?

The semimembranosus sits on the inner back side of your thigh, running from your sitting bone down to attach just below the inner knee. Athletes who do a lot of lateral movements, cyclists, and anyone who sits for long hours with knees slightly bent tend to develop chronic shortening here. When it tightens, you feel it most during deep knee flexion — squatting, climbing stairs, or even just tying your shoe on a bent knee. The tension is very local, very nagging, and weirdly easy to confuse with a joint problem.

The Massage Technique I Actually Use After Coaching Sessions

I sit on the edge of a firm chair, cross my affected leg over the other, and use my thumbs to sink slowly into the muscle belly on the inner back of my thigh — starting mid-thigh and working toward the knee. Short, firm strokes going downward, then small circular pressure right where the muscle narrows near the joint. I spend about four minutes per side, never rushing, and I always pause when I hit a dense knot and just hold pressure for ten seconds. It is not glamorous, but it works better for me than anything a foam roller ever did on that specific spot.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I know if my tightness is actually in the semimembranosus and not somewhere else?

The clearest sign is tightness felt specifically on the inner back side of the knee during bending — not centrally behind the knee and not along the outer thigh. If pressing your thumb along the inner hamstring close to the knee joint reproduces that familiar pulling sensation, the semimembranosus is almost certainly involved.

Can I use Hondrocream on broken or irritated skin?

No — as with any warming cosmetic cream, you should only apply it to clean, intact skin. Avoid areas with cuts, rashes, or any skin irritation, and do a small patch test on your inner arm the first time you use it.

How often should I do this massage routine?

I personally do it four to five times a week after training, and I notice the stiffness stays much more manageable on that schedule. Once or twice a week is still worthwhile if you are short on time — consistency matters more than perfect frequency.

If your inner knee keeps pulling every time you bend deep, give your semimembranosus the attention it has been silently asking for — your future self on the training field will thank you.
