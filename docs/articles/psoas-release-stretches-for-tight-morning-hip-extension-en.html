What the Psoas Actually Is and Why Mornings Are Brutal

The psoas is a deep hip flexor that runs from your lumbar spine to the top of your femur, and it shortens when you sleep curled up or sit for long periods. After a night of relative stillness, it simply hasn't had a chance to lengthen back out. That's why that first step out of bed can feel stiff, wooden, almost stuck. It's not damage — it's just tissue that needs a gentle wake-up call.

Three Psoas Release Stretches I Do Before Coffee

First: a low lunge held for forty-five seconds per side, keeping the back knee on a folded towel if your floors are hard. Second: a supine figure-four stretch — lying on your back, ankle crossed over opposite knee, gently pulling both legs toward your chest. Third: the 90-90 hip stretch on the floor, which I personally think is the most underrated stretch in existence — most people skip it because it looks boring, but nothing opens the hip capsule quite as efficiently. Do all three before you reach for your phone.

Adding a Massage Step That Actually Makes a Difference

I started incorporating a short self-massage on the hip flexor area before stretching, and the difference in tissue readiness is noticeable. Warm tissue responds better — even a two-minute manual pressure routine along the anterior hip and upper thigh changes how the stretch feels. I'm not a morning person, so I need this routine to be fast, and the massage step genuinely earns its place. It's become as automatic as brushing my teeth.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long should I hold each psoas stretch in the morning?

Aim for forty-five seconds minimum per side — research suggests that's around the threshold where connective tissue begins to respond. Going faster is a common mistake; rushing through in twenty seconds gives you almost nothing.

Can I do these stretches if my lower back already hurts in the morning?

Mild morning stiffness in the lower back often responds well to gentle movement, but if you have sharp, persistent, or radiating pain, please see a physiotherapist before starting any new stretch routine — I say this as someone who writes blogs but also sits across from patients every day.
