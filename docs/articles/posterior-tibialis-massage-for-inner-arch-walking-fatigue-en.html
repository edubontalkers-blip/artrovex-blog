Why My Inner Arch Aches After Long Classes — And What I Do About It

Last Thursday I finished a two-hour vinyasa session and could barely walk to my car. Not dramatic pain — just that deep, dull ache along the inner arch that yoga instructors almost never talk about publicly. I have dealt with this on and off for three years, and I finally put together a routine that actually fits my life.

What Is Actually Hurting — The Posterior Tibialis

The posterior tibialis is a muscle that runs deep along the inner lower leg and connects to the inner arch of your foot. When you spend hours barefoot on a mat, cueing balance poses and walking between students, this muscle quietly works overtime. Most people blame their plantar fascia first, but in my experience the tibialis is often the real culprit. Once I started paying attention to it, everything clicked.

My Massage Routine After Evening Classes

I sit on the floor with my leg bent and use my thumbs to work slowly along the inner shin, from just above the ankle upward. I press in small circles, pausing where I feel that tight, almost crunchy tension. I do this for about four minutes per side — no rushing. The key is consistency three or four times a week, not just when things feel bad.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long before I notice less fatigue in my inner arch?

Most people who massage consistently three to four times a week start noticing a difference in how their feet feel after two to three weeks — but that varies completely depending on how much you are on your feet daily.

Can I do this massage before a class instead of after?

I personally prefer after, when the muscle has already worked and feels tight. Before class it can make the area feel almost too relaxed for balance work, but experiment and see what suits your body.
