The Muscle Nobody Talks About: How to Release Your Iliacus for Looser, Freer Hips

If you've been stretching your hip flexors every single day and still feel that deep, stubborn tightness right in the groin area, I want you to meet the iliacus. This little-known muscle sits inside your pelvis and is almost always overlooked — and honestly, once I started paying attention to it, everything changed.

What Is the Iliacus and Why Does It Get So Tight?

The iliacus is a flat, fan-shaped muscle that lines the inside of your hip bone. It works together with the psoas to flex your hip, but unlike the psoas, the iliacus stays tucked deep inside the pelvis where no regular stretch seems to reach it. Sitting for long hours, driving, crossing your legs, or even sleeping in a curled position can keep this muscle in a shortened state for hours at a time. Over days and weeks, that adds up. The result is a feeling of deep groin tightness, a restricted stride when you walk, and that annoying sense that your hip just won't fully open no matter what you do. It's not that your stretches aren't working — it's that you might not be targeting the right spot.

How to Actually Release the Iliacus

The most effective approach combines direct manual pressure with movement. Start by lying on your back with your knees bent. Find the inside edge of your hip bone (your anterior superior iliac spine) and gently sink your fingertips just medial to that point, pressing slowly and carefully inward and slightly downward. You're not digging aggressively — you're sinking in with patience and letting the tissue respond. Hold light pressure for 30 to 60 seconds while taking slow breaths. Then try slowly straightening that same leg along the floor while maintaining gentle contact. This combination of pressure and movement is what makes the difference. Follow this with a low lunge where you actively tuck your pelvis under — this puts the iliacus into a lengthened position. Hold for at least 90 seconds per side to allow the nervous system to actually accept the new range. Do this daily, ideally in the morning before your body has a chance to settle back into its habitual patterns.
