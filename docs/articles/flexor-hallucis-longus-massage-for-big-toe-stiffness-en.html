My Big Toe Was Locking Up After Every Morning Run — Here's What Actually Helped

Three years ago I stepped out of bed one morning and my right big toe simply refused to bend. I had logged maybe twenty years of sprinting, jumping, and heavy squats, so I knew something was happening deep in the foot — not on the surface. That locked feeling sent me straight to my notebook, and eventually to the flexor hallucis longus.

What Is the Flexor Hallucis Longus and Why Should You Care

The flexor hallucis longus — FHL for short — runs from the back of the lower leg all the way to the tip of your big toe. When it gets tight, the toe stiffens, the push-off phase of your walk or run feels wooden, and you start compensating with your knee and hip without even realising it. I have seen this pattern in athletes I coach who are convinced their problem is in the knee. It almost never is.

How I Actually Massage This Muscle

Sit down, cross one ankle over the opposite knee, and find the soft groove just behind your inner ankle bone. Press in with your thumb and slowly flex and extend the big toe — you will feel the tendon moving under your finger. Work up the inner calf in small circles, spending about ninety seconds on any spot that feels dense or resistant. My personal opinion: slow pressure beats fast rubbing every single time on this particular muscle.

Frequently Asked Questions

How often should I massage the flexor hallucis longus?

I do it four to five times a week on the leg I had issues with, and two to three times on the other side as maintenance. Daily is fine if the pressure stays moderate.

Can I do this massage before sport or only after?

I prefer doing it before activity when the toe feels stiff, and again briefly after as part of cool-down. The warming cream makes the pre-activity session feel particularly ready.

My toe stiffness comes and goes — is that normal?

Very common, especially if your footwear changes seasonally or your training load spikes. Consistent massage tends to reduce how often those stiff days appear, in my experience.
