Walking on the Outside of Your Foot? Here's What Your Peroneus Longus Is Trying to Tell You

If you've noticed yourself rolling your weight toward the outer edge of your ankle when you walk, you're definitely not alone — and honestly, it's one of those things most people just push through without ever addressing. That slight shift in your gait can quietly tighten up the peroneus longus, the long muscle that runs along the outside of your lower leg, and over time your whole movement pattern starts to feel stiff and unbalanced. The good news is that a simple, consistent release routine can genuinely change how your foot and ankle feel on a daily basis.

Understanding the Peroneus Longus and Why It Gets So Tight

The peroneus longus runs from just below the outer knee, travels along the outside of the lower leg, wraps under the foot, and anchors near the arch. Its job is to stabilize your ankle and help push off the ground when you walk. When you consistently walk with your weight shifted outward — whether from habit, worn-out footwear, or just the way you're built — this muscle works overtime to compensate. The result is a shortened, overworked muscle that starts pulling on everything around it, including the outer ankle. You might notice a general sense of tightness, your ankle feeling less mobile first thing in the morning, or that odd sensation of your foot wanting to tilt outward even when you're standing still. None of this is dramatic, but it does add up over weeks and months.

A Simple Daily Release Routine You Can Actually Stick To

The release work here doesn't need to be complicated. Start seated with your foot resting on your opposite knee so the outer ankle is accessible. Using your thumbs, apply slow, firm pressure along the outer edge of your lower leg, starting just above the ankle bone and working upward toward the knee. Spend about thirty seconds in any spot that feels particularly stuck or dense before moving on. Next, try the classic peroneus stretch: cross one foot over the opposite knee, hold the top of the foot, and gently invert it so the sole faces slightly upward — you should feel a stretch along the outer lower leg. Hold for twenty to thirty seconds and repeat three times per side. Follow this with ankle circles, both clockwise and counterclockwise, ten repetitions each direction. Do this whole sequence once in the morning and once in the evening, and within a week or two you'll likely notice your foot feels more grounded when you walk.
