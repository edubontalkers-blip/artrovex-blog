My Wrists Were Screaming After Hours of Typing — Here's What Actually Helped

Six months into blogging after my knee surgery, I hit a new wall — my wrists. I was typing four, sometimes five hours a day, and by afternoon my forearms felt like twisted rope. Nobody warned me that recovering from one injury could quietly create another.

What Wrist Pronation Actually Means (And Why It Matters for Typing)

Pronation is simply the rotation of your forearm so your palm faces downward — exactly the position your hands sit in while typing on a flat keyboard. Most people hold that position for hours without a single break or counter-movement. Over time, the muscles responsible for that rotation get stuck in a shortened state, and you feel it as tightness, fatigue, or that dull ache creeping up toward the elbow. It is not dramatic, but it is very persistent.

Three Pronation Drills I Do Every Morning Before I Open the Laptop

First, the Palm-Up Hold: extend one arm forward, flip your palm upward, and hold for 20 seconds. Do this slowly — rushing it defeats the purpose entirely. Second, the Forearm Roll: place your forearm on a desk, then rotate it from palm-down to palm-up ten times, pausing at each end. Third, the Resistance Twist: hold a light water bottle or a small towel, and slowly rotate your wrist against gentle resistance for 15 repetitions each side. In my personal opinion, these three drills done consistently beat any gadget or brace I have ever tried.
