Cuneiform Joint Mobilization: How I Got My Midfoot Moving Again After My Knee Mess

Three years ago I was sitting on a physio table in Madrid, staring at my swollen knee, wondering if I'd ever coach a full training session again. During my recovery, my physiotherapist pointed out something I'd completely ignored — my midfoot was stiff as a brick, and that stiffness was quietly pulling stress upward into my knee. That discovery sent me down a rabbit hole of midfoot work that changed how I move every single day.

What Are the Cuneiform Joints and Why Should You Care?

The cuneiform joints sit in the middle of your foot, connecting three small wedge-shaped bones to each other and to the metatarsals just ahead of them. Most people have never heard of them. But when these joints lose mobility — from tight shoes, years of hard training, or just sitting too much — the entire midfoot becomes rigid. A rigid midfoot means your foot can't absorb load properly, and that shock has to go somewhere.

The Mobilization Routine I Use Three Times a Week

I start seated, foot flat on the floor, and use both thumbs to apply gentle downward pressure across the top of the midfoot while my fingers cradle the sole. Then I introduce small, slow rotational movements — first clockwise, then counter-clockwise — holding each position for about four seconds. I do this for roughly three minutes per foot before any training or morning walk. It sounds boring, but the difference in how my foot feels afterward is genuinely noticeable.

My Honest Opinion on Midfoot Work for Active Adults Over 40

I'll say it plainly — midfoot mobility is the most underrated element in adult fitness. Everyone talks about hips, hamstrings, and shoulders, but nobody mentions the 26 bones packed into each foot. In my personal view, five minutes of cuneiform mobilization is worth more than an extra set of squats for anyone in their forties or fifties. Your feet are the foundation, and foundations need maintenance.
