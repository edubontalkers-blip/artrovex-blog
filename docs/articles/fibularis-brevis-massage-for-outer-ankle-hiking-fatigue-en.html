Last autumn I was descending a rocky trail near Montserrat when that familiar ache started creeping along the outer edge of my right ankle. I know that feeling too well — tight, slightly burning, like something small is being squeezed with every step. After 44 years of living in this body and 20 years guiding wellness workshops, I've learned that the fibularis brevis muscle deserves a lot more attention than most hikers give it.

What Is the Fibularis Brevis and Why Should Hikers Care

The fibularis brevis is a small muscle running along the outer lower leg, attaching right at the base of the fifth metatarsal — that bony bump on the outside of your foot. On uneven trail surfaces, it works constantly to stabilize your ankle with every single footfall. After a long descent especially, it can feel completely wrung out. Most people blame their boots or their knees and ignore this little muscle entirely.

How I Actually Massage This Area After a Hike

I sit down with my foot resting on the opposite knee, which opens up the outer ankle beautifully. Using my thumb, I work in slow circular strokes from just below the outer ankle bone upward along the fibula — not pressing hard, just steady and deliberate. I spend about three minutes on each side, which feels almost meditative after a long trail day. The key is warming the tissue first so the muscle responds instead of guarding.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I know if my outer ankle pain is actually the fibularis brevis?

The most common sign is tenderness right along the outer edge of the ankle and lower leg, especially noticeable when you press just behind and below that outer ankle bone. That said, I'd always recommend seeing a physiotherapist for any persistent or sharp discomfort — massage is a great self-care tool, but it's not a substitute for professional assessment.

Can I use Hondrocream immediately after finishing a hike?

I personally wait about 20–30 minutes after heavy exertion before applying any warming cream, just to let the skin settle and body temperature normalize a bit. After that, Hondrocream works beautifully into the cool-down routine — the immediate warmth it delivers is exactly what you want at that stage.

How much Hondrocream should I use for a fibularis brevis massage?

A pea-sized to almond-sized amount is genuinely enough for this area — you don't need to slather it on. The cream absorbs well, so start small, massage it in fully, and add that second light layer if you want the extended warmth effect.
