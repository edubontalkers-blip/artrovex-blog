Stiff Neck While Driving? These Spinal Rotation Drills Will Change How You Check Your Blind Spots

If you've ever cringed while trying to shoulder-check on the highway, you're not alone. A lot of us spend hours hunched at desks or staring at screens, and by the time we get behind the wheel, our spines have basically forgotten how to rotate. The good news is that a few simple daily drills can genuinely make shoulder-checking feel effortless again.

Why Shoulder-Checking Gets Harder Over Time

Spinal rotation is a use-it-or-lose-it kind of movement. When you sit in the same position for most of the day, the small stabilizing muscles along your thoracic spine — the middle section of your back — get short and sluggish. They stop contributing to rotation the way they should. So when you need to twist around to check a blind spot while driving, your neck ends up doing most of the work on its own, and that's where the stiffness and resistance come from. It's not necessarily an age thing. It's a movement-habit thing. And movement habits can be changed.

Three Spinal Rotation Drills Worth Adding to Your Morning

These drills take about ten minutes total and work best when done consistently, ideally before you get in the car for your commute. First, try the seated thoracic rotation. Sit upright in a firm chair, cross your arms over your chest, and slowly rotate your upper body to the right as far as comfortable, hold for two seconds, then return to center. Repeat ten times on each side. Focus on moving from the mid-back, not just the neck. Second, try the open-book stretch. Lie on your side with your knees stacked and bent at ninety degrees. Extend your arms in front of you, then slowly peel your top arm back toward the floor on the opposite side, letting your chest open up. Go slowly, breathe through it, and aim for ten reps per side. Third, add some standing trunk rotations. Stand with feet hip-width apart, arms relaxed at your sides, and gently swing your upper body from side to side like you're loosely wringing out a towel. This is a warm-up movement, not a stretch — keep it easy and rhythmic for about thirty seconds. Done together every morning, these three drills start waking up the mobility your spine needs for natural, full-range rotation.

Making It a Real Habit That Actually Sticks

The honest truth about mobility work is that it only works if you actually do it regularly. The trick most people miss is attaching these drills to something that already happens every day. A lot of people find it easiest to do them right after brushing their teeth or right before their morning coffee kicks in. Five minutes on the floor, ten minutes in a chair — it genuinely adds up.
