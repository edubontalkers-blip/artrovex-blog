My Throat Was Killing Me After Coaching — Here's the Neck Trick That Helped

Last Tuesday I coached two back-to-back youth football sessions, then ran a two-hour parent briefing. By 9pm my throat felt like I'd swallowed sandpaper — not sore in a sick way, but tight, strained, almost locked. That specific ache around the front of the neck sent me digging into anatomy books I hadn't touched since my knee rehab days.

What Is the Sternohyoid and Why Does It Tighten?

The sternohyoid is a thin strap muscle running from your sternum up to the hyoid bone, sitting quietly at the front of your throat. When you talk for hours — coaching, teaching, presenting — this muscle and its neighbors work constantly without rest. Most people never think about it until the tightness becomes impossible to ignore. I certainly didn't.

A Simple Self-Release Technique I Use Courtside

Sit upright, tilt your chin very slightly upward, and use two fingers to apply gentle, slow downward pressure along the front of your neck, moving from just below the chin toward the collarbone. Hold each spot for about ten seconds, breathe slowly, and never press hard — this area deserves patience, not force. I do this between sessions when I notice my voice starting to fatigue. In my personal opinion, combining this with proper warm-up before heavy talking days makes a noticeable difference in how my throat feels by evening.

Adding Warmth: Why It Matters Before the Release

Cold, stiff tissue doesn't respond well to manual pressure — I learned that the hard way during knee rehab. Applying a warming massage cream to the anterior neck area before the release technique helps the tissue feel more pliable and ready. It's the same logic coaches use before stretching athletes: warmth first, then movement. Nothing complicated, just good sequencing.
