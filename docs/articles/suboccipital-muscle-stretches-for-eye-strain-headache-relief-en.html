Tight Neck, Aching Eyes? Suboccipital Stretches Might Be the Reset You Need

If you spend hours staring at a screen and end up with that dull, pressing headache right behind your eyes, chances are your suboccipital muscles are screaming for attention. These tiny muscles sit at the base of your skull and connect directly to the tissues around your eyes — so when they tighten up, your whole head feels it. I started paying attention to this area a couple of years ago and honestly, it changed the way I deal with long work days.

What Are the Suboccipital Muscles and Why Do They Matter

The suboccipitals are a group of four small muscles nestled right where your skull meets your neck. They control the fine movements of your head — tiny tilts, rotations, micro-adjustments you make constantly without thinking. The problem is that when you hold your head forward toward a screen for hours, these muscles stay contracted the entire time. They never really get a break. Over time, that constant tension can contribute to that familiar tightness that radiates up into your scalp and behind your eyes. Most people never even think about this area until someone points it out, but once you start stretching and releasing it regularly, you notice a real difference in how your whole head feels by the end of the day.

Three Suboccipital Stretches Worth Adding to Your Day

You do not need any equipment for these, just a few minutes and a quiet spot. The first one is the chin tuck. Sit up straight, gently pull your chin straight back — like you are making a double chin — and hold for five seconds. Repeat ten times. It looks silly but it lengthens those muscles beautifully. The second stretch is a gentle neck flexion. Sit tall, let your chin drop slowly toward your chest, and then interlace your fingers behind your head to add very light weight. Hold for twenty to thirty seconds. Do not yank or force it. The third one is a suboccipital release you can do lying down. Place two tennis balls in a sock and lie back so the balls sit on either side of the ridge at the base of your skull. Just rest there for two to three minutes and let gravity do the work. Your neck muscles will slowly soften around the pressure. Try doing these stretches once in the morning and once in the afternoon if you are at a desk all day.
