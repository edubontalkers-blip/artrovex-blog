Last Tuesday I taught back-to-back vinyasa sessions and by the third class my wrists felt like wet cement. I kept pushing through, because that's what we do. What Is the Extensor Carpi Radialis and Why Does It Get So Angry

The extensor carpi radialis — there are actually two of them, longus and brevis — runs along the back of your forearm and is responsible for pulling your wrist back into extension. Every downward dog, every plank, every chaturanga loads this muscle repeatedly. When it accumulates tension without a proper release routine, the whole forearm starts to feel heavy and stiff. It's not dramatic, but it's absolutely draining over a full teaching day.

The Massage Technique I Use on Myself After Heavy Practice Days

I sit with my forearm resting palm-down on my thigh. Using my opposite thumb, I find the muscle belly on the thumb-side of the forearm — roughly two fingers below the elbow crease — and apply slow, firm circular pressure moving toward the wrist. I work the same strip two or three times, pausing on any spot that feels particularly dense. Short strokes, deliberate pressure, no rushing.
