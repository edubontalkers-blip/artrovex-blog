Why Your Side Neck Won't Stop Aching — A Massage Therapist's Honest Take on the SCM

Last Tuesday a client sat down in my chair, turned her head to the left, and winced before she even finished the movement. I knew immediately — the sternocleidomastoid was screaming at her. I've seen that exact expression on probably two hundred faces over the past sixteen years of practice.

What the SCM Actually Is and Why It Gets So Tight

The sternocleidomastoid, or SCM, is that long rope-like muscle running diagonally down the side of your neck. It connects behind your ear all the way down to your collarbone and sternum. Most people have no idea it exists until it starts pulling. Long hours at screens, sleeping on one side, carrying a heavy bag on the same shoulder every day — all of this loads the SCM unevenly over time.

How I Actually Approach SCM Work in a Session

I always start with gentle effleurage along the full length of the muscle before I go anywhere near the deeper tissue. Rushing this step is the most common mistake I see with newer therapists. The SCM runs very close to major blood vessels and nerves, so slow, deliberate pressure with proper feedback from the client is non-negotiable. I usually work with the client's head slightly rotated away from the side I'm addressing, which makes the muscle more accessible without forcing anything.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I massage my own SCM at home?

Yes, but go very gently. Use two fingers and work from just below the ear downward in slow strokes, never pressing hard on the central area of the neck. If you feel pulsing under your fingers, move slightly forward or backward — you've landed on a blood vessel.

How often should SCM massage be done for chronic tightness?

In my practice I usually suggest once a week for three to four weeks when someone has persistent one-sided neck tightness, then we reassess. Daily aggressive self-massage tends to irritate the tissue rather than help it.

Is the cooling sensation from Artrovex cream normal on sensitive skin?

Yes, the initial cooling phase is part of the product's dual-effect design. On very sensitive skin I sometimes apply a thin layer first and wait a few seconds before continuing — clients with reactive skin have responded well to that adjusted approach.

If your neck has been pulling to one side for weeks and nothing has helped, give the SCM some real attention — it's been waiting for you to notice it.
